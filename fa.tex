\section{Taxonomy and Evolution of Edge Caching}
\label{sec:III_unified}

This section organizes the reviewed literature along four axes and traces how each axis developed; Section~\ref{sec:II_background} then turns the axes into the layers of one model. Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the network in which the reviewed caches sit, Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} gives the taxonomy, and Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline} places representative works on one time line.

\subsection{Four Axes and One Device Layer}
Each axis answers one question about the cache decision, and the question fixes which element of the unified caching model (UCM) of Section~\ref{sec:II_background} a work changes, which gives a common way to compare works that use different system models. Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} lists, for each axis, the question, the element changed, the technique families, and representative works. On the people axis we separate SP-side cooperation among base stations, user-side cooperation over D2D links, and the incentives that connect the two. The \emph{controller resource layer} sits under all four axes and asks whether a cache controller can keep the state of the chosen policy within the memory and energy of its node, which matters most for vehicles, roadside units, and microcontroller-class devices. Sections~\ref{sec:IV_time} to~\ref{sec:VIII_devices} review the axes and this layer in turn.

\subsection{How the Field Evolved}
Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline} shows that the four axes did not develop one after another: they overlap in time, and each grew in a different community. The limit results of 2012 to 2016 (Section~\ref{subsec:evolution}) assumed that popularity is known and static, that every node cooperates, that users do not move, and that a hit requires an exact copy, and each axis relaxed one of these assumptions. The time axis went from reactive eviction~\cite{che2002hierarchical} to proactive prediction~\cite{bastug2014living}, bandits~\cite{blasco2014learning,muller2017context}, and, from about 2017, recurrent networks and reinforcement learning~\cite{chen2017echo,sadeghi2018optimal,zhong2018drl}. The people axis went from demand shaping and pricing~\cite{tadrous2015proactive} to Stackelberg games, contracts, and matching~\cite{hamidouche2014matching,chen2016d2dstackelberg,hamidouche2016contracts} and to content markets~\cite{hosny2015marketplace,alotaibi2015game}. The place axis grew from mobility-aware D2D caching~\cite{hosny2016impact,wang2017mobility} into vehicular caching~\cite{alnagar2019city,qiao2020drl}. In this literature the meaning axis started from similarity caching~\cite{chierichetti2009similarity,garetto2020similarity} and was taken up by semantic communication~\cite{xie2021deepsc,gunduz2023beyond}, knowledge and model caching~\cite{yu2023semcache,zheng2024knowledge}, and semantic caches for LLM queries~\cite{bang2023gptcache,gill2024meancache,li2024scalm}.

\subsection{Reading the Taxonomy}
Three observations follow from Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} and Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline}. First, most works sit on one axis and fix the others at the defaults of the first stage: a prediction method is usually tested on a single static cache, and an incentive mechanism usually assumes known popularity. Gains reported along different axes were therefore measured against different baselines and cannot simply be added, since a gain from prediction measured without D2D may shrink or grow once devices share content. This motivates the unified model of Section~\ref{sec:II_background} and the factorial study of Section~\ref{sec:IX_evaluation}. Second, every axis moves toward learning, and larger models must run somewhere, so the controller resource layer constrains all four axes at once. Third, the meaning axis changes the objective. With exact matching a placement is judged by its hit ratio, while with semantic matching it must also be judged by the fidelity of what it serves, so a higher hit ratio is not always better.
